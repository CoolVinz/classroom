# MOD-007 — Shared Classroom Virtual Gallery

**Status:** Gallery service, movement, wand, and expanded avatars are deployed. Walking-shoe and pointer-aiming fixes are covered by regression checks; live school-device verification remains outstanding.
**Coupling:** HIGH
**Risk:** HIGH

## Purpose and behavior

Give each classroom a shared third-person world where teachers and active students can walk among stable student display blocks. Eligible STL/OBJ/GLB/glTF work from active students and assignments appears automatically as 3D geometry only; geometry loads near the visitor and is released when it is far away. Visitors can inspect a model and its student/assignment attribution. Students can like classmates' models once and undo their like. Teachers can hide or restore a display without changing the submission or assessment.

The environment uses a garden-campus palette, low-poly greenery, student terraces, station details, and a Thai entrance sign. The responsive toolbar and grouped character controls use the same compact visual system across desktop and touch layouts.

## Rules and access

- A teacher controls student access for a classroom they own; that teacher can also enter while stopped. Only active linked students can enter an enabled world; anonymous users cannot.
- The first world view assigns missing active students the next available block index. Existing blocks retain their index through roster-number changes, archival, and restoration.
- Active students are placed in ascending classroom-number order, then unnumbered students by name and ID. The persistent `block_index` remains the block identity; physical positions are recalculated from the current roster order. Each space is 12 × 10 units, with four spaces per row, 6-unit side gaps, a 12-unit center aisle, and 7-unit row gaps. Each space shows up to 40 exhibits in a 5 × 8 grid; visitors can page through further loaded work without putting stands in the walkways.
- Each non-empty classroom has one shared mini train. It stops at the entrance and after every three rows, including a final partial group. The track follows the center aisle and returns along the outside. It travels at 22 units/second and waits six seconds at intermediate stops. Students and teachers may board at an open station within five units; the train has 12 seats and riders can leave at any stop.
- Teachers choose automatic departures every one or two minutes, or start each circuit manually. Automatic service begins with a ten-second boarding countdown; after a circuit it waits until both the configured interval and the ten-second post-return minimum are met. Teachers can start at the entrance or choose a numbered student; that student's three-row group station becomes the start. A stale/archived/unnumbered choice falls back to the entrance.
- Colyseus owns train timing, the ordered roster snapshot for each circuit, seat allocation, and rider positions. Schedule changes and roster reordering take effect when the train is empty before its next departure. Seats are released on disconnect. Riding, movement, and train timing do not change attendance or student work.
- Only STL/OBJ/GLB/glTF model and submission files for active students and active assignments appear. Working files, worksheets, assessment data, and other uploads remain private.
- Student entry requires eligible work on the newest active classroom assignment, ordered by creation time and ID. The default `submission` mode admits the linked student after their own final upload, including 3D work saved as `model` and other work saved as `submission`. Teacher uploads and Blender working files are excluded. In `approval` mode, teachers can approve each linked student only after that student submits; approval is bound to the current assignment and student account. Archiving the newest assignment falls back to the next newest active assignment, and deleting the student's qualifying upload revokes entry until another eligible upload exists. With no active assignment, students remain locked. Teachers can enter regardless of student eligibility; the world's existing enabled switch still controls student access.
- The student portal lists enabled worlds with each student's current eligibility and assignment requirement, refreshes status when it regains focus, and links locked students directly to the required assignment. The GalleryWorld access panel lets the owning teacher choose automatic submission access or approval mode and approve/revoke students. App API requests and Colyseus tickets, admission, and periodic session checks all enforce the same database eligibility view.
- Student models hidden by a teacher are omitted from student manifests and content routes. Teachers can restore them from the hidden-work tray.
- Student likes are unique per model/student; self-likes are rejected. Likes do not affect assessment.
- Each classroom has one Colyseus room capped at 50 connections and one connection per account. Room positions are temporary; PostgreSQL retains settings, blocks, hidden displays, and likes.
- Colyseus admission uses a single-use 60-second ticket tied to the existing session. The service validates sessions and membership and rechecks access periodically. Movement is server-bounded and rate-limited.
- WASD/arrows and touch controls are camera-relative: W moves away from the camera and S toward it. The front of the face turns toward accepted travel direction, including diagonals; the server derives facing from accepted movement. Local movement predicts and reconciles server acknowledgements. Space or **กระโดด** starts a fixed-height jump.
- The train display interpolates the last 100 ms of server positions. The train, seated visitors, and their camera use the same interpolated position so they travel together; it holds the last confirmed position during network gaps. The server still controls speed, route, stations, and seats.
- Coarse-pointer devices enter the gallery in landscape when the browser supports orientation locking. If it does not, movement and jumping pause behind a rotate-device prompt until the screen is horizontal. The visible joystick has a moving thumb, and jump starts on touch-down so it works while the joystick is held. Touch input needs a visible page but does not depend on browser focus.
- The world requests no thumbnail images and creates no exhibit image cards or filename plaques. Full model geometry is fetched and parsed one at a time within 50 units, evicted beyond 62 units, and capped at one million resident triangles; individual previews retain the 500,000-triangle and 10-second limits. Unloaded or unavailable slots stay empty, and visitors select loaded geometry directly. Rendering uses pixel ratio 1 without antialiasing and pauses while the page is hidden.
- On the student world page, the existing attendance panel continues to apply its visibility/focus rules. World movement and likes do not affect attendance.
- Teacher and student entry buttons request browser fullscreen while opening the world in the same tab. Direct links and browsers that refuse fullscreen use a full-window view with separate fullscreen and exit controls. Escape exits fullscreen or the fallback view while leaving the room session active. The collapsed menu keeps attendance mounted and contains character customization for both roles, with teacher world settings and display management visible only to teachers; nearby boarding and stopped-train exit actions stay on the play view. On compact and touch screens, the toolbar keeps menu and fullscreen controls visible while room details and return navigation move into the scrollable menu. Its close control stays pinned while scrolling.
- Teachers and students can save one character appearance shared across classrooms, with seven colors, seven hairstyles, four hat styles, three glasses styles, and three shoe styles. The editor and world use the same faceted Three.js model, with five body meshes and 1,040 triangles for the default appearance. Accessories and clothing details merge into the existing body and animated limb meshes. The editor keeps its viewing angle when a choice changes. Colyseus broadcasts appearance across clients, and migration 017 supplies persisted defaults and validation.
- Shoes are merged into each animated leg mesh with vertex colors, preserving their resting position and color without adding draw calls. Walking, jumping, and falling transform the shoes together with their legs.
- Teachers may equip a wand and fire one server-simulated projectile every two seconds. Bolts travel at 20 units/second, stop at 30 units, and use server-derived teacher position, aim validation, movement, and student hit volumes. Duplicate recent shot IDs are ignored. A hit knocks down one unprotected student for ten seconds; they respawn at the entrance with three seconds of visible protection. Knockdown, countdown, protection, and a movement epoch survive reconnects while the room remains alive. Students can still move after respawning; protection only blocks further hits. Combat state is temporary and does not affect attendance, work, likes, or marks.
- Wand crosshairs follow mouse movement or a touchscreen tap within the canvas. Dragging rotates the camera; F or the Fire button casts. Equipping centers the crosshair. Normalized coordinates preserve aim through resizing. The camera ray targets the nearest intersected student character (excluding name labels, shields, and scenery), otherwise a point 30 units along the camera ray; the resulting direction is measured from the existing muzzle origin. Elevation beyond the server's `abs(dy) <= 0.75` limit shows an unavailable crosshair and disables firing. Customization, hidden tabs, lost focus, and disconnected rooms block firing; pointer cancellation and lost capture stop camera drags.
- OBJ files may include one UTF-8 MTL companion with solid `Kd` colors. Material maps are ignored and never fetched; students can see material colors in previews, saved thumbnails, and the world. Texture files and Blender shader materials are unsupported.
- Movement uses local prediction with server input acknowledgements. Small server corrections decay visually over several frames, with the camera following the same corrected display position; corrections above two units snap immediately. Local walking animation follows input rather than correction distance. The gallery caps pixel ratio at one, avoids antialiasing, and pauses animation and model loading in hidden tabs. These changes reduce avoidable work; measured FPS on school devices is still unknown.
- Appearance writes use JSON objects in PostgreSQL, preserving selections on a failed save. Movement pauses in the editor and when the page loses focus. Display lists synchronize when their data changes rather than on each animation frame; removed characters release geometry and materials.

## Warnings and open questions

- Coolify uses a separate single-instance service for `gallery-server`, pointed at `gallery-server/Dockerfile`, plus `GALLERY_WS_URL` on the app. Configure its health check at `/__healthcheck`. Production WSS, real student accounts, simultaneous users, and VPS load have not been verified.
- Realtime service rooms are in-memory on one instance. Adding replicas requires shared Colyseus presence/matchmaking and load-balancer configuration.
- FPS has not been measured on school devices, under 50 connected visitors, or in a gallery with hundreds of displays. A device may still stutter when it renders many blocks or avatars.
- Train timing, station boarding, and the 50-connected-visitor train flow have not been verified against a live Colyseus room. The train is simulated in one in-memory service instance; restarting that service resets the current route and passenger seats while preserving teacher schedule settings.
- Coursework screens may use saved previews. In the world, unavailable or over-budget model geometry leaves an empty slot; MTL texture maps and external resources are ignored. Model and companion files stay in the app's persistent upload volume.
- Network latency, school WebSocket filtering, fullscreen and orientation-lock support, and browser graphics performance may vary by device. Some mobile browsers allow only the full-window fallback or require manual rotation.
- The wand is a temporary classroom game feature. Rate limits and hit detection are server-authoritative, but live multiplayer timing, school-device rendering cost, and the student hit/respawn flow have not been browser-verified.
- Shoe transforms, colors, draw-call counts, and disposal have regression coverage. Cursor rays are tested at multiple camera angles and viewport sizes. Local Chrome checks with synthetic avatars cover mouse/F casting, synthetic touch taps and drags, pointer cancellation/lost capture, and customization suspension. Real touch hardware and school-device performance remain unverified.

## Start/stop and glTF

Teachers start/stop student access with the existing enabled setting. The owning teacher may inspect a stopped world. Students cannot enter, fetch gallery content, or like displays while stopped. Active rooms check status every two seconds and disconnect students with code 4403; restart does not automatically reconnect them.

GLB and embedded glTF 2.0 use the shared worker and Three.js loader. External resource URIs, Draco/Meshopt/KTX2 compression, and unsupported required extensions are rejected. Default scenes render statically with transforms, materials, and embedded PNG/JPEG textures. Decoded images are limited to 16 million pixels per model; the world caps resident texture allocations including mipmaps at 128 MiB and releases textures with distant geometry. Existing triangle/time limits remain.

Additional code: src/gltf.ts, web/src/parse-gltf-model.ts. Run bun test ./src/gallery-world.integration.ts separately with CLASSROOM_TEST_DATABASE_URL pointing to an isolated migrated database; it starts a local Colyseus server and verifies stop/restart, stale tickets, and teacher access.
